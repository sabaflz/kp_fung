# Bet #3 partner update: what to say

Every word below is meant to be said out loud. There are no stage
directions in the script itself, so any of it can be read straight off
the screen without editing as you go. Delivery notes are at the end,
kept separate on purpose.

This is the same text that is in the deck's presenter notes, generated
from them so the two cannot drift apart. You do not need this open while
presenting. It is here to rehearse from and to send to Derek and Ali.

**Numbers are from Thursday night**: 19 responses, 18 in range, dental
60 percent, therapy and mental health 53 percent. The slides read live

About 8 minutes 5 seconds at a steady speaking pace.

## 1. Opening

*About 24 seconds.*

So this week we're moving from which benefits people say they want, to whether any of it actually gets someone to do something.

I'll go through what Bet 2 is telling us so far, what we built off the back of it, and then we have three things we'd like your help with at the end.

## 2. Where Bet 2 landed

*About 93 seconds.*

Quick bit of context first. In Bet 1, all five participants chose an allowance they could control over a fixed bundle, so that gave us the shape. Bet 2 was about what goes inside it.

We're at 19 responses now, 18 of them in our 18 to 30 range.

Dental is leading. The average person picked the package with dental in it about 60 percent of the time. Therapy and mental health is second at 53. After that the rest are fairly close together.

Two things about that before anyone has to ask. First, 19 is a small sample, and the gap between dental and therapy sits inside the margin of error. So we're treating this as a direction rather than a result.

Second, how to read these numbers at all. Each screen puts three benefits against the other three, so the six rates always average 50 percent by design. A benefit below 50 isn't unwanted, it just showed up less often in the packages people picked.

And on our own success criteria: we said we'd want to see one benefit clearly outperforming the others, and four out of five people favouring the same one. Dental is leading, but we haven't hit four out of five, and at this sample size I wouldn't call the lead settled either.

## 3. A category is not a benefit

*About 72 seconds.*

That brings me to the thing we kept running into, which is really why Bet 3 looks the way it does.

More responses would make that ranking sharper. What they wouldn't do is tell us what dental actually means to somebody. It could be a cleaning, it could be help paying for a filling, it could be a crown they've been putting off for two years. Those are three different things to build, and you can't build any of them from the word.

Your feedback on the fitness benefit is what made that click for us. The way we'd worded it read like a gym membership, and what we meant was broader, anything that keeps someone moving, so classes, a climbing pass, a mobility program. And if it read that way to you, it almost certainly read that way to the people taking our survey.

So we stopped trying to fix a wording problem by asking people about wording, and built something they could react to instead.

## 4. What we built

*About 101 seconds.*

We built two pieces that work together.

The first is a flyer. It's a 300 dollar a year allowance, and the three benefits on it come straight from the survey, so dental, therapy and mental health, and everyday health. If the ranking changes, the flyer changes with it.

Instead of just naming categories, it gives examples. Cleanings and help with fillings, therapy sessions, everyday things like cold medicine and first aid. We made those examples up ourselves, and they're exactly where we need your eyes.

The second piece is the page it links to, and this is the part I'd really point at. It doesn't go straight to the survey, it gives four ways in: what's included, who could get it, how it would work, and is this real. Which one somebody opens first tells us what they needed to know before they'd go any further, and that's a question we couldn't really ask them directly.

We can see how many arrive, which way in they pick, how long it takes them, and whether they start the survey or leave an email, all broken down by platform.

And on the privacy side, since it'll probably come up: we don't collect IP addresses, we don't use cookies, and there's nothing that follows anyone anywhere. The page says plainly that this doesn't exist yet and nobody's selling it, and there's no KP branding on any of it.

## 5. Bet 3

*About 102 seconds.*

So here's the bet itself, which the three of us agreed on yesterday.

If we show someone the flyer with a clear next step, at least four out of five people in our age range will click through within a minute, without us prompting them. We're counting the click, not whether they say they like it.

We're running it as two separate tracks.

The first is the actual bet. Five people in our age range that we recruit ourselves. We hand them the flyer, say take a look, and then say nothing. That's the group we judge against four out of five.

The second is public. The same flyer on Instagram, Discord, Snapchat and Facebook, each with its own link so we can see which platform does what. That gives us reach and a click rate, but it can't tell us that whoever clicked was in our age range.

We're keeping those apart on purpose, because a normal click-through rate on social is one to three percent. Held to four out of five, a perfectly good flyer would look like a failure, and that isn't useful to report.

One limit I want to be upfront about. A click doesn't prove anyone would buy this, it proves the offer is worth thirty seconds. And if people look, say it's nice and do nothing, that's still the cheapest thing we could learn, because it's before anyone spends money building it.

## 6. What we need from you

*About 93 seconds.*

So, three things we'd like from you, and each one changes what we do over the next two weeks.

The first is telling us what this can't claim. It goes out this week, and we've written things about covered services and access that we're honestly not qualified to promise. We'd much rather hear that now than after five hundred people have read it.

The second is fifteen minutes with whoever knows benefit design, starting with dental. What a realistic amount looks like, what the limits would be, what somebody actually gets. That one conversation is the difference between a ranked category and something you could build. If you can point us at a name today, we'll handle the scheduling.

The third one is a bit uncomfortable to ask, but we'd rather ask it now than at the final. How does this sit next to KP Rewards? We've seen flexible dollars and Gen Z in your own material, and if that's the same mechanism, we'd rather build on it than end up presenting something beside it.

And next time we meet we'll come back with numbers. The five person test, the public response, and which part of the offer people opened first.

If you can start with the flyer claims though, that's the one that's blocking us.

## Delivery notes

These are for you, not for the room.

**Before you start.** Have `/plan` open in a browser tab. In section 4
you want to show the real page rather than the miniature on the slide,
and that is not something to go looking for live.

**Section 2, the numbers.** Read them off the screen rather than from
this page, because the chart is live and will have moved. The success
criteria sentence at the end of that section is written to stay true
whether or not dental has separated from the pack, but if the dashboard
has shifted to say there is a clear leader, say so.

**Section 3, the fitness wording.** Credit the room, not one person by
name. Everyone on that call contributed, and singling one person out
implies the others did not.

**Section 5, the bar.** It is agreed, so it is "we set the bar at", not
"we were thinking". Say the one to three percent figure yourself, before
anyone assumes a low social number means the flyer flopped.

**Section 6, the asks.** Do not let these soften into a wish list. On
the second one, get an actual name before the call ends and offer to do
the scheduling yourself, or it becomes "we will look into it".

**If you are running long**, the opening and the privacy paragraph in
section 4 are the two places to cut. Do not cut the sample size caveat
or the two tracks.

## If you are asked

**How many responses do you need?** Enough that the leader separates
from a coin flip. We will say when it has, and we will say if it has
not.

**Why no price?** Asking someone what they would pay for something that
does not exist leads them. If we need a price signal it has to come from
behaviour rather than from a question.

**Is this a real KP offer?** No, and the materials say so themselves. No
branding, no price, and the page states plainly that it does not exist.

**What if nobody clicks?** Then we have learned the offer is not
compelling before anyone spent money building it, and we still have the
ranking and the interviews.

**Can you break the results down by age?** Not yet in any meaningful
way. Almost everyone who has answered is 21 to 23, so there is no
comparison to make between age bands inside this sample.
