# Bet #3 partner update: spoken script

Six slides, about seven and a half minutes, which leaves room for one
question without running over.

Say this in your own words. What is worth keeping is the order, the
landing lines, and the places where you get ahead of a question instead
of waiting for it. Reading it aloud will sound like reading.

The same script is in the deck's presenter notes, so you do not need
this open while presenting.

---

## 1. Title, 20 seconds

We know which benefits people reach for. What we do not know is whether
any of it would make someone actually do something. That is this week.

Three minutes on what Bet #2 says, two on what we built, three asks for
you at the end.

> Do not recap the three bets. They have been on every call with you.
> That recap belongs at the final, where the room is wider.

## 2. Where Bet #2 landed, 90 seconds

Bet #1 told us the shape: five out of five chose an allowance they
control over a fixed bundle. Bet #2 asked what goes in it.

> Read the counts off the screen. They are live and they will have moved
> since you last looked.

Dental leads. The average person picked the package with dental in it
about sixty percent of the time. Therapy and mental health is second.
After that it is tight.

Two things to say plainly before anyone asks.

One: this is a small sample. Dental leads, but the gap to second is
inside the noise. We are calling it a direction, not a result.

Two: our own bar for this bet was four out of five people favouring the
same benefit, and we have not hit it.

> Say that yourself. It is on the dashboard in plain words, and it is
> much better coming from you than found by them.

And one quirk of the design, because it changes how you read every
number on there: each screen puts three benefits against three, so the
six rates average fifty by construction. A category under fifty is
behind the field, not unwanted.

## 3. A category is not a benefit, 75 seconds

Here is what we kept running into. More responses would sharpen that
ranking. They would not tell us what dental means.

Someone picking dental might mean cleanings, might mean help with a
filling, might mean a crown they have been putting off for two years.
Those are three different products, and you cannot build from the word.

Your feedback on the fitness benefit is what made this concrete for us.
The wording read as a gym membership. We meant anything that keeps
someone moving: classes, a climbing pass, a mobility program. If it read
that way to you, it read that way to the people we surveyed.

**You cannot fix a wording problem by asking people about wording. So we
stopped asking and built the thing.**

> Credit the room, never one person by name.

## 4. An offer, and somewhere for it to go, 90 seconds

> Have `/plan` already open in a tab. Show the real page, not the
> miniature on the slide.

Two pieces. First a flyer: three hundred dollars a year, and the three
benefits on it come straight from the survey, so if the ranking moves
the flyer moves with it. It gives examples rather than categories.
Cleanings and help with fillings, therapy sessions, everyday things like
cold medicine and first aid.

We invented those examples. They are exactly where we need your eyes.

Then the part I would point at. The flyer does not link to the survey.
It links to a page with four ways in: what is included, who could get
it, how it would work, and is this real. **Which one somebody opens
first tells us what they needed to know before they would go further.**
That is the question we could not ask them directly.

We can see arrivals, which way in they chose, how long before they
moved, and whether they started the survey or left an email. Per
platform.

> Get ahead of the privacy question rather than waiting for it.

What we do not collect: no IP address, no cookie, nothing that follows
anyone anywhere. The page says in its own words that this does not exist
and nobody is selling it, and there is no KP branding on any of it.

## 5. Does it make anyone move, 90 seconds

> The bar is agreed, so say "we set the bar at", not "we were thinking".

Shown the flyer with a clear next step, at least four of five target
users click through within a minute, unprompted. We count the click, not
whether they say they like it.

Two tracks, and we will report them apart.

Track one is the bet. Five people in range that we recruit ourselves. We
hand it over, say take a look, and say nothing else. That is the group
the four-of-five judges.

Track two is public: Instagram, Discord, Snapchat, Facebook, each with
its own link. That gives us reach and a click rate per platform. It
cannot prove that anyone who clicked was in range.

We keep them apart because a click-through rate on social is normally
one to three percent. Held against four of five, a perfectly good flyer
looks like a failure, and we are not going to report it that way.

> Say the one-to-three-percent figure yourself, before anyone assumes a
> low social number means the flyer flopped.

One honest limit. A click does not prove anyone would buy this. It
proves the offer is worth thirty seconds. And if people look, say it is
nice, and do nothing, **that is the cheapest thing we could possibly
learn right now.**

> Close on that. A partner who hears you welcome a bad result trusts the
> good one more.

## 6. Three things that change what we do next, 90 seconds

Three asks, and each one changes what we do in the next two weeks.

> Do not phrase these as questions. "Could you help us" gets "we will
> look into it."

**First, tell us what this cannot claim.** It goes out this week. We
have written things about covered services and access that we are not
qualified to promise, and we would rather hear it now than after five
hundred people have read it.

**Second, fifteen minutes with whoever knows benefit design.** Dental is
the place to start. What is a realistic amount, what are the limits,
what does a person actually get. That one conversation is the difference
between a ranked category and something you could build.

> Ask for a name in the room, and offer to do the scheduling yourself.

**Third, the uncomfortable one.** How does this sit next to KP rewards?
We have seen flexible dollars and Gen Z in your own material. If that is
the same mechanism, we would rather find out now and build on it than
present it beside it at the final.

Next time we meet, you get numbers: the five-person test, the public
response, and which part of the offer people opened first.

Starting with the flyer claims, because that one is blocking us.

---

## If you are asked

**"How many responses do you need?"** Enough that the leader separates
from a coin flip. We will say when it has, and we will say if it has
not.

**"Why no price?"** Asking someone what they would pay for something
that does not exist leads them. If we need a price signal it has to come
from behaviour, not from a question.

**"Is this a real KP offer?"** No, and the materials say so themselves.
No branding, no price, and the page states that it does not exist.

**"What if nobody clicks?"** Then we have learned the offer is not
compelling before anyone spent money building it, and we would still
have the ranking and the interviews.
