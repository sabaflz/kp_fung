# Final presentation: story and roadmap

Draft. Every claim below is written to be defensible with evidence we
actually hold, and the gaps are marked rather than smoothed over. The team
needs to agree the arc before anyone builds slides.

## The one sentence

We set out to find the supplemental benefit package 18 to 30 year olds
would actually choose, and we found that asking them to choose between
whole packages tells you something that asking them to rate benefits does
not.

## The arc

Four moves, each one caused by what the previous one failed to settle.

**Bet 1. People will name what they want.** We interviewed, and they
named the things everybody names. Interviews told us the vocabulary of the
problem and could not rank anything, because nobody turns down a benefit
in the abstract.

**Bet 2. Forced choice will rank it.** So we made them trade. Ten
head-to-head screens, two $300 packages each, every benefit tested against
every other an equal number of times. This is the methodological heart of
the project and the part worth presenting slowly.

**Bet 3. A real-looking offer will show whether anyone acts.** A stated
preference in a survey is not demand. So we built the package into a flyer
that looks like something an insurer would send, put it in front of people
where they actually are, and measured whether they take a next step.

**The recommendation.** What we would tell someone designing this
benefit, and how confident they should be in each part of it.

## What the evidence supports, claim by claim

Honest column second. This is the table to rehearse, because it is where
questions will come from.

| What we say | What stands behind it |
| --- | --- |
| Forced choice separates benefits that flat ratings do not | The method itself, and the spread in the results. Strong. |
| Dental leads | 59% win rate across 16 people. Leads, but 7 points over therapy and not yet separable from a coin flip. |
| Dental belongs in the package | All five most-picked packages contain it. This is the stronger form of the claim and we should lead with it. |
| No single benefit has cleared Bet #2's 4-of-5 bar | True, and we should say it before anyone asks. Note all three bets set a bar of 4 of 5 and they measure different things: Bet #1 was whether people name a benefit they would prioritise, and it was met; Bet #2 is whether 4 in 5 favour the same one; Bet #3 is whether 4 in 5 click through. Never say "our 4-of-5 bar" without the number. |
| Respondents are the right people | 15 of 16 were inside 18 to 30, and 18 to 30 is the default view everywhere on the site. |
| Something in the flyer makes people act | Bet 3, in flight. Nothing to claim yet. |

Two things we must not say. We cannot claim an age-group difference: this
batch is almost entirely 21 to 23. And we cannot name a price, because we
deliberately never asked, on David's advice that price questions lead the
witness.

## What the gap in the middle is for

The most useful slide in the deck is the one that admits the survey
ranked categories and not specifics. "Dental" is not a benefit anyone can
build. What dental, up to what amount, for whom. That gap is exactly what
Bet 3 and the five remaining interviews are for, and framing it as the
designed next step rather than an oversight is the difference between a
project that knows what it is doing and one that ran out of time.

## Before the final

- Bet 3 numbers in, with the supervised and social tracks reported
  separately. The bar, agreed by the team on Nov 12, is 4 of 5 bullseye
  users clicking through within a minute, and it judges the supervised
  track only. A 1 to 3% social click rate is a normal result, not a
  failure, and is never measured against that bar.
- Five more interviews, aimed at specifics inside the leading benefits
  rather than at ranking them again.
- Enough survey responses that the leader separates from a coin flip, or
  an explicit statement that it has not.
- A decision on the interest list. People left their address believing a
  student research team would send them results. If any of it reaches KP,
  the survey has to say so first.
- Partner review of the slides, so the numbers are not seen for the first
  time in the room.

## After the class

The honest version of "what happens next", which is a better ending than
a fake launch plan.

**What we hand over.** The method, the instrument, and the data. The
survey is reusable: another team can point it at a different age group and
get a comparable ranking, which is worth more than our 16 responses.

**What we would do with another month.** Close the specifics gap inside
the top two benefits, and get the sample large enough to rank the middle
of the pack rather than just the leader.

**What we would tell KP to do first.** Test the leading package as a real
option against the plans they already sell, rather than as a survey. We
can say what people choose on paper. Only they can find out what people
buy.
