## Where Bet #2 landed

19 responses so far, 18 of them aged 18 to 30.

| Benefit | Picked |
| --- | --- |
| Dental | 60% |
| Therapy and mental health | 53% |
| The other four | clustered close behind |

Two caveats we would rather state than have you find.

**The sample is small.** The gap between dental and therapy sits inside
the margin of error. We are treating this as a direction, not a result.

**The rates always average 50% by design.** Each screen puts three
benefits against the other three, so a benefit below 50% appeared less
often in the packages people chose. It is behind the field, not
unwanted.

Against our own success criteria, which were one benefit clearly
outperforming the others and four out of five people favouring the same
one, we have a leader and we do not yet have the agreement.

## Why Bet #3 is not simply more survey responses

More responses would sharpen the ranking. They would not tell us what
dental means to somebody: a cleaning, help with a filling, or a crown
they have been putting off. Those are three different things to build,
and a ranked category is not something anyone can cost.

Your feedback on the fitness benefit is what made this concrete for us.
Our wording read as a gym membership when we meant anything that keeps
someone moving. If it read that way to you, it read that way to our
respondents, and that is not a problem we can fix by asking people more
questions about wording.

## What we built, and what we are testing

A flyer, attached, and a page it links to at
`kp-fung-saffreon.vercel.app/plan`.

The page offers four ways in: what is included, who could get it, how it
would work, and is this real. Which one somebody opens first tells us
what they needed to know before going further.

**The bet.** Shown the flyer with a clear next step, at least four of
five people aged 18 to 30 click through within a minute, unprompted. We
count the click, not whether they say they like it.

**Two tracks, reported separately.** Five people we recruit and watch,
which is what the four-of-five judges, and public posts on Instagram,
Discord, Snapchat and Facebook, which give reach and a click rate per
platform but cannot confirm who clicked. A normal social click-through
rate is one to three percent, so holding a public post to four of five
would make a good flyer look like a failure.

On privacy: no IP addresses, no cookies, nothing that follows anyone.
The page states plainly that the benefit does not exist and nobody is
selling it, and none of the materials carry KP branding.
